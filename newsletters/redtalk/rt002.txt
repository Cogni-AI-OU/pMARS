The undiscovered area between LP and 94draft                           by Fizmo
Part II:  Scanner vs. Paper/Imps


This time I will show the impact of -p between scanner and paper/imps. For this
reason I run every scanner (scn) from the fsh94nopv0.3 against the paper/imps
(pwi).

Well, my first impression is that the defensive power of a paper/imp strongly 
depends on the max. process setting. As more processes available as more strong 
the imps. 

Let's see first how well Recon 2 performs. It is one of the best warriors
to beat paper/imps.

Recon 2
-p      %W      %L      %T
16000   57.4    29.1    13.5
8000    57.1    29.1    13.8
4000    34.3    29.7    36.0
2000    18.1    31.1    50.8
1000    18.3    32.8    48.9
500     24.3    35.4    40.3
200     40.0    36.7    23.3
100     53.7    34.5    11.8

Woahh, that's by far not what I expected. Down to -p 200 Recon loses most of
the offensiv power, with a minimum around -p 1000-2000. Just at -p 100 it
scores similar to -p 8000.

Let's see first how P. Kline's He Scans Alone performs against the 12 paper/imps:

He Scans Alone
-p      %W      %L      %T
16000   42.5    37.5    20.0
8000    42.0    39.8    18.2
4000    40.0    44.1    15.9
2000    38.6    46.1    15.3
1000    38.6    47.0    14.4
500     40.4    44.4    15.2
200     43.9    42.2    13.9
100     48.3    37.6    14.1

Also He Scans Alone shows a similar behaviour than Recon 2 but much less
pronounced. The minimum percentage of wins is again around -p 1000-2000.
The same can be found also Mischief, The Marsupial Lion, Qutrum, Razor,
Solo 3, Willow and Win.

The only exceptions are Agony II and Deathstar, which shows a sligh
increase in wins with decreasing max. processes down to -p 500. Below
this they start to perform well.

Deathstar
-p      %W      %L      %T
16000   21.1    40.4    38.5
8000    21.1    40.8    38.1
4000    20.8    41.1    38.1
2000    23.3    40.0    36.7
1000    27.5    37.3    35.2
500     33.3    34.6    32.1
200     47.9    30.7    21.4
100     55.5    27.8    16.7


Overall one can say, that paper/imps gain strength with decreasing max.
processes. Just below -p 500 they start to become vulnerable against
scanner. 

Next time I will review the third strategy pair. As more I am working on
this as more I am curious.

The Random Warrior Talk                                              Drawn v1.2


This time I picked B. Cook's Drawn v1.2. It was sent some years ago onto the
Sourceforge Corewars Beginner Hill. The strategy line claims a Paper-launched 
Imp Ring. Cool, that are my 'pet subjects' in corewar, papers and imps ;-)
At a first glance on can see that it is a 'pure' warrior without any boot, 
decoy or qscan. The paper itself is the so called 'silk' style, i.e. splitting 
before copying. 

However, this warrior contains some uncommon code I haven't seen before in a 
paper, so I'm curious to make a deeper look into the code.

;redcode-94
;name Drawn v1.2
;author B. Cook
;strategy Paper-launched Imp Ring
;assert 1

org     start

step    equ     1143

cnst1   equ     2393    ;apparently magic
cnst2   equ     2341    ;prime numbers

start   spl     1,      0
        mov.i   -1,     0
        mov.i   -1,     0
        spl     1,      0

silk1   spl     @0,     cnst1
        mov.i   }silk1, >silk1
silk2   spl     @0,     cnst2
        mov.i   }silk2, >silk2
lawn    spl     2,      <0
go      jmp     @0,     imp
sprd    add.ab  #step,  go
        mov.i   die,    {go
die     dat     <0,     <lawn
imp     mov.i   #step,  }0

        end


Let's have a look to the first four lines. They do nothing else than generating 
parallel processes. This is needed to run a silk-style paper properly. The number 
of parallel processes is usually similar to the number of replicated lines.

0000*SPL.B  $1,       $0       
0001 MOV.I  $-1,      $0       
0002 MOV.I  $-1,      $0       
0003 SPL.B  $1,       $0       

There is a common rule for the parallel process generation. Simply converting the 
number required in binary 3 -> 11, subtract one -> 10, use a spl 1 for every one 
and a mov -1,0 for every zero. In this case we have a 1001, which generates 10
parallel processes.

The next two lines is the first of the two silk pairs. They copy the whole code to 
a different location and start to execute them there.

0004 SPL.B  @0,       $2393    
0005 MOV.I  }-1,      >-1      
    
Line 0004 split at the a-field address i.e. the address pointed by b-field zero 
locations away, the b-field of the line they are executing, 2393 locations away. 
When all ten processes executed this line we have ten other processes ready to 
execute line 0004+2393, there is nothing to execute here but the twenty processes 
executes now in alternating order. I suggest to follow this e.g. in Corewin to 
get a better understanding what the spl instruction is doing. Line 0005 move 
what's pointed by the a-field of line 0004 to the location pointed by b-field of 
line 0004 then they increment both a and b field of line 0004. This means, that 
the first of the twenty process moves line 0004 2393 cells away from line 0004 
and leaves line 0004 changed such a way:

0004 SPL.B  @1,       $2394

The second process executes the line 0004+2393 which isn't empty anymore now, while 
the third process executes again line 0005. This copies now line 0005 2394 cells 
away from 0004, just after the previous line. 

The same happens with line 0006 and 0007 for another location:

0006 SPL.B  @0,       $2341    
0007 MOV.I  }-1,      >-1  


The 5 last lines belongs to a jmp/add-launcher which generates a 7-pts imp spiral
with 10 processes:


0008 SPL.B  $2        <0       
0009 JMP.B  @0        $4       
0010 ADD.AB #1143     $-1      
0011 MOV.I  $1        {-2      
0012 DAT.F  <0        <-4      
0013 MOV.I  #1143     }0       

Line 0011 is quite interesting as it wipes the already executed code and leaves
just the jmp/add-launcher for the imps. Line 0012 finally terminates the
ten processes of the paper.

Let's see how well he do against the different strategies. For that I've 
choosen the fsh94nopv0.3 benchmark from optimax:

pap: Paper; pwi: paper with imps; pws: stone/paper
scn: scanner; cds: clear directing scanner; clr: coreclears, oneshots
stn: bomber, vampires; sai: stone/a-imps; sbi: stone/b-imps

Strategy   %W      %L      %T     Score
pap        0.6    27.9    71.5     73.3
pwi        0.2    12.1    87.7     87.7
pws        1.8    39.6    58.5     64.0
scn       15.8    72.5    11.7     59.0
cds       17.6    65.6    16.8     69.5
clr       13.8    68.0    18.2     59.5
stn       17.0    58.3    24.7     75.7
sai        1.7    30.2    68.1     73.2
sbi        2.7    28.8    68.6     76.6

Well, from the ties one can see that the replication and the imps work as 
expected, but it clearly misses some offensive power. One could add a
quickscanner for some easy extra wins or launch it together with a second, 
more agressive paper. Also a combination with a stone would be worth to try.

Overall one can say that it's a nicely written, unique warrior with some good 
potential for improvements.
