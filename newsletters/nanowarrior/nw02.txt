Using GP to Evolve for the Corewar Nano Hill by Giovanni Squillero

About GP
---------

GP (aka. ugp, MicroGP) is a tool for automatically generating assembly
programs.  It exploits evolutionary techniques, that is, it mimics some
principles of the Darwinian theory of natural selections to improve and
refine a set of solutions.

GP was originally developed in Politecnico di Torino (CAD Group) in 2002.
Recently, it has been completely redesigned.  The NEW version (named
MicroGP++, or ugp3) is available to the public through sourceforge:
http://sourceforge.net/projects/ugp3/

Copyright (c) 2002-2007 Giovanni Squillero
GP source code is distributed under GNU Public License (GPL) and the
documentation under GNU Free Documentation License (FDL).

A Brief Introduction to GP's Evolutionary Techniques
-----------------------------------------------------

The GP cultivates a population of individuals in evolutionary steps named
generations.  The first population may be loaded from a file or generated
randomly.  At every generation a variable number of new individuals is
generated by manipulating the existing ones.  All of these are evaluated
and added to the existing population.  This is subsequently sorted in
descending fitness order and truncated to the initial size, discarding all
the worst warriors.

Individuals are composed of a sequence of nodes, each one corresponding to
a redcode instruction (although this correspondence is not strictly
required).

The GP uses several evolutionary operators to manipulate its individuals:
insertion of a new random node; deletion of an existing node; random
mutation of one of the node parameters; local mutation of one parameter;
scan mutation; crossover.  Of these the scan mutation deserves a special
discussion.

Warriors for the nano hill are very small programs, whose functioning
depends strictly upon the exact values of all their constants.  It makes
sense, then, to be able to fine-tune any one of them in the search for an
optimum.  While a local mutation exists, the strong nonlinearity of the
fitness function makes a long-range search more effective.  The scan
mutation answers exactly that need, allowing to find the (local) best value
for a given parameter, even when the fitness function is very rough.

The fitness function plays a fundamental role in every evolutionary
approach.  Fitness must be able to lead the evolution toward the desired
goal, or at least away from the less promising region of the search space.

The chosen fitness function depends on the points earned by the warrior
against all programs in the test hill.  However, test warriors were first
ranked, and the points earned by the warrior against all programs were
weighted considering the relative strength of the opponent.

The idea behind this approach is to favor warriors able to compete well
with strong warriors.  However, the ranking is able to measure only the
relative strength, and since these warriors are not a significant sample
of the SAL nano hill it could be useless.

Extra Extra: Rumpelstiltskin

A great deal of discussion followed the arrival of two new warriors on
SAL's nano hill in early December 2006.  Impossible and Rufus both scored
~40% ties and it was commonly believed gnik had found a successful
implementation of a nano paper/imp.

In reality Impossible and Rufus are both imp ring launchers as is the most
successful of gnik's warriors, rumpelstiltskin, which entered the hill in
January 2007:

;redcode-nano
;name rumpelstiltskin
;author gnik
;strategy "Today I bake, tomorrow brew,
;strategy The next I'll have the young Queen's child.
;strategy Ha! glad am I that no one knew
;strategy That Rumpelstiltskin I am styled."
;assert CORESIZE==80

s1 EQU 14
s2 EQU 46
s3 EQU 18
s4 EQU 3
s5 EQU 10
istep EQU 27

        org     go
first   spl     imp+2*istep, >s1
go      spl     1,           >s2
        spl     first,       >s3
        spl     imp+istep,   <s4
imp     mov.i   #s5,         istep
        end

Rumpelstiltskin launches one true imp ring (3 consecutive processes) and a
number of fractured imp rings (non-consecutive processes).  During Bvowk's
attempt to completely wipe the nano hill, Rumpelstiltskin fell after
attaining an age of 797.

For the purpose of comparison, I've created several nano imp-launchers
using a number of different layouts.

Legend of the ice dragons combines the techniques used by Die Hard and
Return of the Fugitive to launch imp spirals while performing a partial
clear:

;redcode-nano
;name legend of the ice dragons
;author John Metcalf
;strategy imp spirals
;assert CORESIZE==80

     istep equ 27

     spl   #imp+istep*2, >19
     mov   }imp,         {-1
     spl   imp-istep,    >-34
     spl   imp+istep,    >39
imp: mov.i #-1,          istep
     end

Realm of the fire flower's launcher builds imp spirals using a method
inspired by Return of the Fugitive:

;redcode-nano
;name realm of the fire flower
;author John Metcalf
;strategy imp spirals
;assert CORESIZE==80

     istep equ 27

     spl   #9,           >-10
     spl   istep+1,      >-15
     mov   -1,           istep
     mov   -1,           istep
imp: mov.i #14,          istep
     end

Scythe creates fractured imp rings and performs a partial spl-wipe:

;redcode-nano
;name scythe
;author John Metcalf
;strategy imp rings
;assert CORESIZE==80

     istep equ 27

     mov   <37,          18
     mov   boot,         >imp-istep*2
boot:spl   imp-istep*2,  <12
     spl   -2,           <-32
imp: mov.i #10,          istep
     end

War of the snowflakes attempts to combine an anti-imp clear with a
Return of the Fugitive inspired imp ring launcher:

;redcode-nano
;name war of the snowflakes
;author John Metcalf
;strategy imp rings
;assert CORESIZE==80

     istep equ 27

clr: mov.i #1,           <1
     spl   clr,          55
boot:spl   imp-istep*2,  >41
     mov   boot,         imp-istep*2
imp: mov.i #13,          istep
     end   boot-1

Strategy: An Increment Resistant Clear

While experimenting with nano clears, we discovered a certain type of
clear which can recover from damage caused by either a or b increments:

     mov   > ?      ,   ?
     djn.f < 0      , > 0

This snippet has been used to create clears with a stream of increments
which passes right through the clear without causing any harm.  An
additional benefit is the invisibility of the djn.f to f-scanners.

If we single step through the source as the stream of increments reaches
the code, first we see the increment damage the djn.f (2) followed by the
djn.f repairing itself (3).  * indicates the next instuction to execute:

(1)  mov   > 1      ,   ?  ; *
     djn.f < 0      , > 0

(2)  mov   > 1      ,   ?
     djn.f < 0      , > 1  ; *

(3)  mov   > 1      ,   ?
     djn.f < 0      , > 0  ; *

(4)  mov   > 0      ,   ?  ; *
     djn.f < 0      , > 0

You may be interested in comparing the following variation:

     mov   { ?      ,   ?
     djn.f { 0      , } 0

The first (successful) warrior to use this technique was a twin clear by
Neogryzor/FatalC.  The twin clear can be an effective strategy, so it's
surprising to see so few on the nano hill:

;redcode-nano
;name early morning view
;author Neogryzor/FatalC
;strategy double clear
;assert CORESIZE == 80

        mov.i   clear+1     ,    {boot
        mov.i   clear       ,    {boot
boot    spl     clear+40    ,    }15
clear   mov.i   >-19        ,    -2
        djn.f   <0          ,    >0
        end

The increment resistant clear also works well as part of a qscan, as
demonstrated in wreath of thistles:

;redcode-nano
;name wreath of thistles
;author John Metcalf
;strategy qscan
;assert CORESIZE==80

     x     equ 17
     y     equ 61

     sne   ptr+x*y-5,  ptr+x*y-10
     seq   {adj,       ptr+x*y-y-6
adj: mul   #x,         ptr
ptr: mov   >-35,       y
     djn.f <0,         >0
     end

And for our last example, here's the clear used as part of a typical
nano clear/imp:

;redcode-nano
;name spawn of the dark core
;author John Metcalf
;strategy clear/imp
;assert CORESIZE==80

clr: spl   #40,        >-39
     mov.i >-22,       -6
     djn.f <0,         >0
star:spl   clr,        >-35
     mov.i #1,         1
     end   star

Strategy: Nano Scanner/Imp

      <Fluffy> I need a good (!) for a new warrior
      <Fluffy> Any suggestions?
      <Nenad> scanner/imp :)

When Nenad suggested creating a scanner/imp in the #corewars IRC channel,
it's unlikely he expected Fluffy to rise to the challenge.

The first nano scanner/imp, Snowy Skies by John Metcalf, entered the hill
in December 2006 and Fluffy immediately set about making improvements.
The resulting warrior, lord of the digital swamp, achieved an age of 296:

;redcode-nano
;name lord of the digital swamp
;author Metcalf/Fluffy
;strategy scanner/imp
;assert CORESIZE==80

      step  equ 75
      first equ ptr+72

      add    # step,    ptr
ptr:  jmz.f  -1,        first
      mov.i  # 1,       @ ptr
      spl    ptr,       > ptr
      mov.i  # 1,       1

      end    ptr

LotDS has a similar structure to a typical nano scanner.  For example, see
Type-1 in nanoWarrior issue 1.  The anti-clear bomb (mov.i #1, @-1) has
been modified and now forms part of the scanner's attack loop.

When LotDS's scan loop detects something, control passes to the attack
loop. For each location bombed, the attack loop splits off a new process
and allows a process to fall through into the imp.

Inspired by the ;strategy line in Snowy Skies, inversed independently
developed his own scanner/imp, Xinyl.  The anti-clear bomb used by Xinyl
appears to be slightly more effective than the variety used by LotDS:

;redcode-nano
;name Xinyl
;author inversed
;strategy Scanner/Imp
;assert CORESIZE==80

step    equ     -5
zofs    equ     -9
aval    equ     4
bval    equ     27

x0      equ     loop
org     loop+1

loop    add     #step,          ptr
ptr     jmz.f   loop,           x0+zofs

        mov.i   #aval,          >ptr
        spl     ptr,            >bval
        mov.i   #1,             1

And finally, silver ribbon is an example of an effective OoS-style
scanner/imp.  The attack is included within the scan loop and bombs every
6th location while the scan detects nothing.  When something is detected
the attack switches to bombing consecutive locations and splitting off imps:

;redcode-nano
;name silver ribbon
;author John Metcalf
;strategy scanner/imp
;assert CORESIZE==80

ptr: add   #5,       #11
wipe:mov.i #1,       >ptr
     jmz.f ptr,      @ptr
     spl   wipe,     {-10
     mov.i #1,       1
     end   wipe+1
